# Research Report on Common Network Security Threats

## Introduction

Network security threats are malicious activities that target computer networks, systems, and data. These attacks can lead to data theft, service disruption, unauthorized access, and financial loss. Understanding common threats and their countermeasures is essential for protecting digital infrastructure.

---

# 1. Denial of Service (DoS) Attack

## What is a DoS Attack?

A Denial of Service (DoS) attack is an attack where a system, server, or network is flooded with excessive traffic or requests, making it unavailable to legitimate users.

---

## How It Works

The attacker sends a huge number of requests to the target system. The server becomes overloaded and cannot respond to genuine users.

---

## Impact

- Website downtime
- Service disruption
- Financial losses
- Reduced customer trust

---

## Real-World Example

In 2016, the Dyn DNS attack disrupted major websites like Twitter, Netflix, and Reddit using massive traffic generated by IoT botnets.

---

## Prevention and Mitigation

- Use firewalls
- Deploy Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS)
- Use rate limiting
- Configure DDoS protection services
- Monitor unusual traffic patterns

---

# 2. Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) Attack

## What is a MITM Attack?

A Man-in-the-Middle attack occurs when an attacker secretly intercepts communication between two parties.

---

## How It Works

The attacker places themselves between the victim and the server, capturing or modifying data during transmission.

---

## Impact

- Theft of passwords
- Session hijacking
- Data manipulation
- Identity theft

---

## Real-World Example

Attackers on unsecured public Wi-Fi networks often intercept user traffic to steal login credentials and personal information.

---

## Prevention and Mitigation

- Use HTTPS encryption
- Use VPNs
- Avoid public Wi-Fi for sensitive tasks
- Enable multi-factor authentication
- Use secure network protocols

---

# 3. Spoofing Attack

## What is Spoofing?

Spoofing is an attack where an attacker pretends to be a trusted device, user, or service.

---

## Types of Spoofing

- IP Spoofing
- ARP Spoofing
- Email Spoofing
- DNS Spoofing

---

## How It Works

Attackers forge network information or identities to trick victims into trusting malicious traffic.

---

## Impact

- Unauthorized access
- Data theft
- Malware distribution
- Network redirection

---

## Real-World Example

Phishing emails often use email spoofing to appear as trusted companies like banks or online services.

---

## Prevention and Mitigation

- Use email filtering
- Implement packet filtering
- Enable network authentication
- Use DNS security extensions (DNSSEC)
- Educate users about phishing attacks

---

# 4. Phishing Attack

## What is Phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering attack where attackers trick users into revealing sensitive information.

---

## How It Works

Victims receive fake emails, messages, or websites designed to look legitimate.

---

## Impact

- Credential theft
- Financial fraud
- Malware infections

---

## Prevention

- User awareness training
- Email filtering
- Multi-factor authentication
- Verify suspicious links

---

# 5. Malware Attacks

## What is Malware?

Malware is malicious software designed to damage systems or steal data.

---

## Types of Malware

- Viruses
- Worms
- Trojans
- Ransomware
- Spyware

---

## Impact

- Data loss
- System compromise
- Financial damage

---

## Prevention

- Use antivirus software
- Keep systems updated
- Avoid suspicious downloads
- Regular backups

---

# Conclusion

Network security threats continue to evolve and become more sophisticated. Organizations and individuals must implement strong security practices, update systems regularly, and educate users about cyber threats. Preventive measures such as encryption, firewalls, intrusion detection systems, and security awareness can significantly reduce risks.
